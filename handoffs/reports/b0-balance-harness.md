# B0 shared balance harness

Status: corrected by root after the owner explicitly reopened the stopped task; fresh independent review PASS. Branch: `build/b0-balance-harness`, integrated with current main. Historical failed gates remain in `b0-review.md`; the final correction review is `b0-resource-review.md`.

The repository-only tool is `scripts/balance-sim.mjs`, exposed as `npm run balance`. It imports production engine functions and `mulberry`, `hero`, and the three established policies from `tests/sim-helpers.mjs`. It uses an injected clock and seeded RNG and does not import campaign storage or UI modules.

Fixtures accept `kit`, `level`, `race`, `gear`, `relics`, ability `mastery`, `abilities`, or an idle serialized `save` fixture. `gear.mainHand` aliases the production `weapon` slot. `gear.secondHand` maps to the current `offhand` slot and must name a real compatible item such as `shield`; a second weapon is rejected because the game engine has no second-weapon rules. Relic ranks, future family mastery, race traits, menders, unknown fixture fields, and unknown CLI/config fields fail before simulation rather than yielding a baseline-looking row. Seeds must be unique safe integers, bounded at 10,000; chain length is bounded at 50; a matrix is bounded at 100,000 seeded encounters. Each seed owns a continuous RNG stream, so its result is independent of the ordering of other seeds. The CLI defaults to one fixture/enemy/policy, 40 seeds and a 10-fight chain.

Each seed starts one chain at full Stuffing, Sparkle, and Bounce. Those resources and production Pluck carry persist across victories in that chain, with no rest, camp, or between-fight refill. A defeat records one loss and ends that chain. The per-fight net loss is the HP at that encounter's start minus post-action HP after actual healing, including the relic heal on victory; it is not clamped to zero. `winRate` is wins divided by started encounters, and rows state seed count, chain length, total encounters, wins, defeats, and completed chains. Fixture XP is set to zero on the simulation clone before the chain begins, and a victory that nevertheless levels the fixture is rejected without producing a row. Level, stats, points, and mastery remain fixed across fights; HP/MP/SP remain exactly as resolved by the engine, including final-blow and victory relic effects. The source fixture and its progression metadata are never modified. The simulator does not claim campaign coin, XP, loot, achievement, or story-claim rewards as part of a chain. This keeps each cell's build fixed while preserving resource and relic carry.

Ordinary encounters use `startFight(..., 'hunt')` after setting prior claimed chapter receipts on the disposable fixture. Chapter 1's boss uses its valid municipal delve node; Chapter 2–5 bosses use a valid synthetic accepted finale contract with all non-boss objectives satisfied through production state. Scripted bosses are single-encounter cells because their contracts cannot be farmed. Chapter 1's real delve victory auto-rest is excluded from the net Stuffing-loss metric using pre-rest HP plus actual exchange and victory healing. No resources are carried after a scripted boss because it is a single-encounter cell. Ordinary victories use actual engine HP/MP/SP without reconstruction.

The Markdown table distinguishes expected `actionProfile.average`, engine attempted damage, enemy HP actually removed, and overkill. Landed damage includes the enemy HP delta from relic reflection. Mend and relic healing use actual result fields; Cocoa and Patchwork Heart victory heals are counted from the exact engine log receipts, never inferred from resource deltas that could include level-ups or delve auto-rest. Mender and trait healing render `not measured`, because those mechanics do not exist in this engine. The sustain policy prioritizes a finishing strike, answers a telegraphed heavy with Disrupt or a guard, uses a useful available Mend, and uses Recover when the selected spell cannot be afforded. `actionCounts` exposes the policy's actual verbs for tests and analysis.
