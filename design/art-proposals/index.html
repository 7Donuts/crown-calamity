Visual exploration · 2026

Crown & Calamity

Five possible visual languages for a dark, text-first canal-city RPG. The same subjects appear in every board so the styles are easy to compare.

Earlier exploration: 01 · Etched Brass. The newer Soft Pixel study follows feedback asking for simpler, softer art. This earlier board’s engraved linework and restrained brass-and-teal palette connect scenes, creatures, equipment, and interface ornament. Simplify fine hatching for small mobile displays.

Common brief

Bracken Quay canal-side weapon stall
Paper-scrap Ledger Rat enemy
Sword, round shield, and magic focus
Rune-frame ornament and three small gear or merchant glyphs

All boards use a dark umber, antique brass, verdigris teal, and parchment palette. No player portraits or readable interface copy. Images are concept proposals, not selected production art.

01 · Etched Brass Earlier pick

Feel: intricate ink engraving, tactile and old-world.

Strength: one coherent visual language spans settings, enemies, items, and interface details.

Tradeoff: dense hatching needs simplification at small sizes.

Feel: atmospheric gouache fantasy with warm lamplight.

03 · Runic Projection

Feel: precise teal magic lines on dark surfaces, grounded by brass.

Strength: gives spells and magical interface details a distinct identity.

Tradeoff: too much glow could overwhelm ordinary gear and the medieval setting.

04 · Illuminated Ledger

Feel: manuscript illumination, flat pigments, and gold-like ornament.

Strength: symbolic shapes support a strong in-world bestiary and ledger identity.

Tradeoff: dense decoration may crowd narrow layouts and creature variety.

05 · Pixel Relic

Feel: crisp limited-palette sprites with dark bronze and teal.

Strength: clear small-scale silhouettes and a repeatable sprite language.

Tradeoff: commits every new asset and animation to consistent pixel scale and palette.
