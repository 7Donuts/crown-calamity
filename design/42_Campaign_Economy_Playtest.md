# Campaign economy playtest

This note reports seeded offline engine fixtures. It does not describe observed human play. Work is paused with an unapproved retry-aware candidate in PR #13. Earlier policy matrices are documented below; the latest candidate has regression tests but no final same-source 40-seed matrix or independent review. Run `npm run economy` for the bounded JSON report; the simulation is isolated in `scripts/weapon-economy.mjs` and never runs during gameplay.

## What the fixture does

The earned walkthrough starts a fresh level-1 character for each kit and seed with the normal 20 coins. It calls the production contract, story-action, delve, combat, XP, rest, purchase, equipment, and receipt APIs. It meets Mara and follows the records route below the Laundry Works. The current policy allocates seven earned points to Vitality, then later points to the kit's primary stat. Before the Laundry Works dive it takes legal same-level hunts until level 8; this is a policy choice, not a new contract requirement. At safe points it learns/equips Mend and Disrupt when eligible, uses production `actionInfo` and `actionProfile` for Recover and heavy responses, uses the explicit Spin Cycle Vent when available, and buys/equips affordable useful gear through the production preview/receipt APIs. The kit's shield/charm/focus setup is preserved. It can equip useful owned drops, never buys a chapter-ceiling weapon, and sells nothing on the earned route. After defeat it waits through the wound, uses actual rest, records the lost coins and allows one retry per contract; a second defeat ends that run. No campaign receipts, gear, coins, or stats are granted by the fixture. A paired branch keeps its coins for comparison.

The separate region-farm fixture intentionally sets a chapter receipt and chapter-cap level to represent a mature character. It uses the best current-region defensive pieces and the previous chapter's best same-channel weapon (the normal starter weapon in Chapter 1), then repeats that region's highest-level ordinary enemy. It saves toward that chapter's highest-tier weapon, so the target item is not already owned. Its receipt and level are synthetic setup, not walkthrough rewards. The distinct C2 tier-10 preparation metric forks a production-reloaded state at the earned Arcade finale receipt and continues against accessible C2 foes with the actual carried coins, XP, equipment, and inventory. Its sale branch sells each eligible ID once per sale pass through production `sell`; a new copy dropped later can be sold on a later pass. The report separates initial checkpoint-inventory proceeds from reacquired-drop sale flow, and records the actual sale receipts alongside encounter rewards. This measures post-receipt preparation, not the five-chapter route.

Timing uses 1.5 seconds per combat/story/delve/claim input, 2.5 seconds per hunt for opening, results and navigation, four seconds for each explicit rest plus its start/finish inputs, and 1.5 seconds per sale. The report also projects 2.5-second and four-second response intervals for counted inputs. The campaign route records each defeat and its production penalty, then allows one wound wait/rest/retry per contract; it stops after a second defeat in that contract. For an ordinary failed hunt, the model charges the 2.5-second result/navigation allowance once after the required wound/rest recovery and before retry or stop. This is a conservative placement assumption that avoids overlapping the allowance with the 12-second wound wait; it is a model allowance, not a new engine rule. C1 delve scene transitions keep their own action timing and do not receive this hunt overhead. The T10 continuation has a hard 10,000-fight limit and permits one production recovery retry in its post-receipt phase. Reading, breaks, exploration, time choosing equipment, and shop-menu dwell are outside these estimates.

## Current observations

The deterministic catalog audit covers all 145 tiered items, including monotonic prices and the rule that regional loot cannot come from a later chapter. G1-B CP1 now uses a piecewise trial curve: weapon T1/T10/T15/T20/T25 prices are 100/10,000/15,000/30,000/60,000; body armor T6/T8/T10 prices are 3,750/7,500/15,000 with the same multiplier applied to the other armor categories. At the provisional pooled earned ordinary-income rates, the C3/C4/C5 best weapons represent 11.39/11.22/10.60 modeled active hours at the 2.5-second input pace. T25 costs about 1,408 C5 wins and remains within the spec 50 300–2,000-win best-gear band. C5's seven-slot armor basket costs 81,250 and the full weapon-plus-armor purchase basket costs 141,250, about 24.96 modeled hours. These are CP1 trial numbers, not a passed campaign balance gate.

The seeded mature-region samples suggest the Chapter 2 tier-10 weapon is in the approved long-play range under slower input intervals. The new Chapter 3–5 targets were selected from provisional earned-income walkthrough rates, not the separate synthetic region-farm projections. Treat all timings as model estimates; they do not establish measured human sessions or final campaign pacing.

The owner preapproved CP2 and CP3 direction. CP4 still needs fitted-input evidence and review; the CP1 merge alone does not approve CP4 or the earned campaign balance.

The previous five-Vitality, no-retry policy completed the five receipts in 33/40 Breaker, 40/40 Duelist, and 23/40 Caster runs. Caster failures were concentrated at the Chapter 1 Laundry Works boss; later Caster losses and individual Breaker failures are also preserved in the raw results. A seven-Vitality/no-retry diagnostic completed 40/40 Breaker, 40/40 Duelist and 20/40 Caster runs. The retry-aware seven-Vitality candidate has not received its final 40-run comparison. Regression cases cover Caster seed 2 completing without a death, seed 4 completing after a logged boss death/rest/retry, and seed 9 stopping after its one retry and second boss death. Another case charges the ordinary-fight result overhead once outside wound/rest time. Tests check that no coins are refunded, defeat penalties close in the ledger, and spent plus unspent attributes equal exactly the four points awarded per level. These individual seeds do not establish the two-thirds gate; the candidate matrix is pending.

The actual C2 tier-10 continuation reloads the earned C2 finale save, including its carried coins, level, gear, and inventory. The first recorded seed-1 continuation used the previous five-Vitality policy and pre-CP1 prices: no-sale Breaker/Duelist/Caster bought the 10,000-coin weapon after 1,420/1,489/1,429 ordinary wins and 8,520/8,934/8,574 encounter coins, taking 422.8/436.7/426.1 modeled minutes after the receipt. In the sale branch, they realized 1,746/1,881/1,707 coins from sale receipts and bought after 1,129/1,176/1,145 wins and 6,774/7,056/6,870 encounter coins, taking 346.0/356.8/351.4 minutes. The initial checkpoint inventory contributes 162/159/159 coins; the remaining 1,584/1,722/1,548 coins come from drops reacquired and sold on later passes. At the 2.5-second counted-input pace, total new-character-to-purchase estimates are 725.9/737.9/733.9 minutes without sales and 622.0/630.6/632.9 with them; at four seconds they are 999.6/1,012.6/1,011.7 and 855.2/864.3/871.3 minutes. These one-seed offline results are preliminary and use the previous allocation policy and prices. Carried C2 cash is counted once. Sale-branch proceeds include ongoing repeat-drop sales, so they do not represent finite checkpoint loot or encounter-only sustainable income. Rerun these measurements on the merged CP1 price tree before using them as current campaign input. The longer paced estimates exceed the aspirational 8–12 active hours for some kits/branches and should be reviewed without changing the input pace or adding unapproved objectives.

The synthetic farm fixture records defeats and marks a run incomplete if it cannot sustain the selected enemy. This is one combat policy and one seed, useful for identifying encounters to inspect rather than as a universal win rate.

- Use each receipt checkpoint's interval wins, ordinary encounter coins, scripted-boss/delve rewards, contract awards, purchases, defeats, equipment, and sale previews to verify whether the CP1 trial curve fits the earned route.
- Recompute C2 T10 timing from the final earned checkpoint policy, compare with the 8–12 active-hour aspiration, and compare C1 receipt time with the 1–2 hour target. Keep the documented input pace and report any mismatch.
- Complete CP4 post-story fit before treating the Chapter 3–5 prices or entry target as final balance.
- Rerun the synthetic region samples after gear, enemy, XP, payout, rest, or resale changes; keep them separate from earned campaign progression.
