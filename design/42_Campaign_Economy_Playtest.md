# Campaign economy playtest

This note reports seeded offline engine fixtures. It does not describe observed human play. Work is paused with an unapproved retry-aware candidate in PR #13. Earlier policy matrices are documented below; the latest candidate has regression tests but no final same-source 40-seed matrix or independent review. Run `npm run economy` for the bounded JSON report; the simulation is isolated in `scripts/weapon-economy.mjs` and never runs during gameplay.

## What the fixture does

The earned walkthrough starts a fresh level-1 character for each kit and seed with the normal 20 coins. It calls the production contract, story-action, delve, combat, XP, rest, purchase, equipment, and receipt APIs. It meets Mara and follows the records route below the Laundry Works. The current policy allocates seven earned points to Vitality, then later points to the kit's primary stat. Before the Laundry Works dive it takes legal same-level hunts until level 8; this is a policy choice, not a new contract requirement. At safe points it learns/equips Mend and Disrupt when eligible, uses production `actionInfo` and `actionProfile` for Recover and heavy responses, uses the explicit Spin Cycle Vent when available, and buys/equips affordable useful gear through the production preview/receipt APIs. The kit's shield/charm/focus setup is preserved. It can equip useful owned drops, never buys a chapter-ceiling weapon, and sells nothing on the earned route. After defeat it waits through the wound, uses actual rest, records the lost coins and allows one retry per contract; a second defeat ends that run. No campaign receipts, gear, coins, or stats are granted by the fixture. A paired branch keeps its coins for comparison.

The separate region-farm fixture intentionally sets a chapter receipt and chapter-cap level to represent a mature character. It uses the best current-region defensive pieces and the previous chapter's best same-channel weapon (the normal starter weapon in Chapter 1), then repeats that region's highest-level ordinary enemy. It saves toward that chapter's highest-tier weapon, so the target item is not already owned. Its receipt and level are synthetic setup, not walkthrough rewards. The distinct C2 tier-10 preparation metric forks a production-reloaded state at the earned Arcade finale receipt and continues against accessible C2 foes with the actual carried coins, XP, equipment, and inventory. Its sale branch sells each eligible ID once per sale pass through production `sell`; a new copy dropped later can be sold on a later pass. The report separates initial checkpoint-inventory proceeds from reacquired-drop sale flow, and records the actual sale receipts alongside encounter rewards. This measures post-receipt preparation, not the five-chapter route.

Timing uses 1.5 seconds per combat/story/delve/claim input, 2.5 seconds per hunt for opening, results and navigation, four seconds for each explicit rest plus its start/finish inputs, and 1.5 seconds per sale. The report also projects 2.5-second and four-second response intervals for counted inputs. The campaign route records each defeat and its production penalty, then allows one wound wait/rest/retry per contract; it stops after a second defeat in that contract. The T10 continuation has a hard 10,000-fight limit and permits one production recovery retry in its post-receipt phase. Reading, breaks, exploration, time choosing equipment, and shop-menu dwell are outside these estimates.

## Current observations

The deterministic catalog audit passes for all 145 tiered items, including monotonic prices and the rule that regional loot cannot come from a later chapter. The weapon prices reach 100 at tier 1, 10,000 at tier 10, 129,155 at tier 15, 1,668,101 at tier 20, and 21,544,347 at tier 25. The resale cap prevents repeated low-value drops from becoming a sustainable source for these prices.

The seeded mature-region samples suggest the Chapter 2 tier-10 weapon is in the approved long-play range under slower input intervals. Chapters 3–5 top-tier weapons outpace recurring region rewards by a wide margin. Treat all farm timings as estimates from synthetic, manually prepared regional fixtures; they do not establish earned campaign pacing.

The previous five-Vitality, no-retry policy completed the five receipts in 33/40 Breaker, 40/40 Duelist, and 23/40 Caster runs. Caster failures were concentrated at the Chapter 1 Laundry Works boss; later Caster losses and individual Breaker failures are also preserved in the raw results. A seven-Vitality/no-retry diagnostic completed 40/40 Breaker, 40/40 Duelist and 20/40 Caster runs. A later seven-Vitality candidate with one actual retry per contract has not received its final 40-run comparison. Regression cases cover a previously failing Caster seed (seed 2) completing all receipts without a death, seed 9 completing after its logged boss death/rest/retry, and seed 4 stopping after its one retry and second boss death. The tests check that the wound wait and rest are counted, no coins are refunded, defeat penalties close in the ledger, and spent plus unspent attributes equal exactly the four points awarded per level. Those individual seeds do not establish the two-thirds gate; the candidate matrix is pending.

The actual C2 tier-10 continuation reloads the earned C2 finale save, including its carried coins, level, gear, and inventory. The first recorded seed-1 continuation used the previous five-Vitality policy: no-sale Breaker/Duelist/Caster bought the 10,000-coin weapon after 1,420/1,489/1,429 ordinary wins and 8,520/8,934/8,574 encounter coins, taking 422.8/436.7/426.1 modeled minutes after the receipt. In the sale branch, they realized 1,746/1,881/1,707 coins from sale receipts and bought after 1,129/1,176/1,145 wins and 6,774/7,056/6,870 encounter coins, taking 346.0/356.8/351.4 minutes. The initial checkpoint inventory contributes 162/159/159 coins; the remaining 1,584/1,722/1,548 coins come from drops reacquired and sold on later passes. At the 2.5-second counted-input pace, total new-character-to-purchase estimates are 725.9/737.9/733.9 minutes without sales and 622.0/630.6/632.9 with them; at four seconds they are 999.6/1,012.6/1,011.7 and 855.2/864.3/871.3 minutes. These one-seed offline results are preliminary and use the previous allocation policy. Carried C2 cash is counted once. Sale-branch proceeds include ongoing repeat-drop sales, so they do not represent finite checkpoint loot or encounter-only sustainable income. The longer paced estimates exceed the aspirational 8–12 active hours for some kits/branches and should be reviewed without changing the input pace or adding unapproved objectives.

The synthetic farm fixture records defeats and marks a run incomplete if it cannot sustain the selected enemy. This is one combat policy and one seed, useful for identifying encounters to inspect rather than as a universal win rate.

## Follow-up before calling the campaign balanced

- Complete the candidate seven-Vitality 40-seed earned matrix, compare with the published five-Vitality baseline, and report all failures and both purchase policies by receipt.
- Use each receipt checkpoint's interval wins, ordinary encounter coins, scripted-boss/delve rewards, contract awards, purchases, and one-time sale preview to inform the price analysis.
- Revisit Chapter 3–5 weapon prices or recurring rewards. The current price curve appears too steep for later top-tier weapons to function as ordinary campaign goals.
- Recompute C2 T10 timing from the final earned checkpoint policy, compare with the 8–12 active-hour aspiration, and compare C1 receipt time against the 1–2 hour target. Report any mismatch; do not alter input pace or add unapproved objectives to make targets fit.
- Rerun the region samples after any gear, enemy, XP, payout, rest or resale changes. Keep synthetic region setup and post-receipt preparation separate from earned campaign completion time.
