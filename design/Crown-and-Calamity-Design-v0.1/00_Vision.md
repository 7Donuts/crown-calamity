# Vision and boundaries

Status: rough draft. This version deliberately focuses on the solo game loop, levels 1–10, character building and the browser experience. Company, kingdom and PvP ideas below are long-term context only.

## What the player actually does

The player chooses a target or contract, prepares a build, explicitly initiates individual combat exchanges, receives readable results, and spends the proceeds on a better or stranger build. Routine hunts can be repeated comfortably; marked adventures offer more direct decisions. Repeating that loop opens regional stories, deeper dungeons and company ambitions. The attraction is understanding a system and becoming impressive within it.

The character is the primary unit. The opening game should stand on the quality of its solo loop and sheet. The relationship to future armies, companies or territory has not been designed yet.

The primary interface is a portrait phone browser. The compact, tap-driven combat loop and character screens must work comfortably there before desktop adaptation. A larger screen is optional, never the assumed way to manage a build.

## Design pillars

**Builds should have verbs.** A durable caster should redirect attacks, store energy or turn a successful guard into a spell opportunity. Merely combining a large health pool with a generic fireball is not enough.

**Every reward needs an attributable action.** Being logged in, waiting for a bar, owning land or leaving a tab running awards no experience, gold, items, mastery, troops or prestige. Players can still read, trade, chat and roleplay at their own pace.

**Depth should be available without constant effort.** A prepared build should handle familiar hunts with little deliberation. Marked dangerous encounters can ask whether to guard, interrupt, heal or finish an objective. Players choose when to seek that additional attention.

**Numbers should explain the fiction.** Equipment has requirements because a body must handle its weight or a mind must stabilize its runes. Prestige requires evidence of controlled power, not hundreds of repetitions of a trivial task.

**The world can be absurd without becoming disposable.** A dragon can have a municipal job. Its fire and the town it threatens still matter. Comedy belongs in complications and commentary, while characters retain emotional stakes.

## Moment-to-moment promises

An ordinary player can finish something useful in five to ten minutes. A longer session can complete a dungeon, test a build or help a company. A break does not erase progress or create a debt of missed login rewards.

Basic resource recovery and unlocked safe travel are immediate explicit actions outside combat. Neither requires waiting nor generates progress. Challenge comes from resource limits inside an expedition.

## A small amount of setting to test the tone

Candidate premise: the world is being repaired through magical contracts issued by a damaged administrator. The first dungeon is failed civic infrastructure whose safety system has become hostile. Achievements act as a permanent, frequently embarrassing record of how a person actually plays. Test one city and one dungeon before building a large atlas.

The proposed voice is bureaucratic disaster comedy. It is an original tone and premise, rather than borrowed dialogue, characters or distinctive running jokes from the named books.

## User constraints and proposed boundaries

- No character images, avatars, paper dolls or generated portraits. A heraldic company symbol is not a character likeness.
- No class selection, permanent class trees or hidden class locks disguised as specializations.
- No automatic initiation of future battles, auto-repeat, hold-to-farm, offline expeditions, passive training, resource ticks or paid action regeneration. One tap resolves one exchange; it does not resolve the entire fight.
- No rewarding click speed, keystroke count or time merely spent logged in.
- No real-time movement or aim requirement.
- Future PvP fairness and monetization remain later decisions, not current implementation tasks.
- Proposed narrator rule: rewards follow game events and defined budgets rather than discretionary stat gifts.

## Session examples

**A relaxed session:** Select a familiar hunt, repeat familiar combat actions through exchanges, watch XP/materials accumulate, occasionally recover or compare a useful drop, and stop whenever desired.

**A focused session:** Clear a short solo delve with marked decision points, earn materials toward a selected item, discover a skill interaction and receive an unusual achievement.

**Thirty minutes:** Explore the solo delve, decide between a safer route and a material-rich route, return to change equipment, and retry the boss with a better plan.

These are usability targets. Actual times depend on reading speed, network behavior and the final number of decisions. They are not promised leveling rates.
