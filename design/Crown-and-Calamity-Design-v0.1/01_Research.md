# Research: what to inherit, adapt and replace

Reviewed 28 October 2026. These are document-based findings, not claims from a hands-on playtest. Official RWK help spans different servers and includes legacy interface descriptions. Its current announcements demonstrate ongoing changes. Do not assume that every archived number describes the live game today.

## Race War Kingdoms

The official starter guide describes selecting a creature, initiating combat and choosing weapon, spell, combined or defensive actions. It connects creature fighting to gold, levels and equipment replacement. [R1]

The official terms describe separate attributes for physical damage, hitting, evasion, health, spell output, casting success and spell interference. Experience can come from damage dealt rather than the last hit. [R2]

The actions reference describes shared attacks on active monsters, XP diverted into skills, crafting and enchanting. It also describes claiming kingdoms, taxation of kills, morale pressure, armies, fortification and attacks on other kingdoms. [R3]

**Design inference:** RWK's useful foundation is the short hunt–improve–hunt loop connected to a shared geographic and political layer. Preserve quick access to fights, build experimentation and group targets. Modernize the interface, make mechanics inspectable, and replace repetitive low-information inputs with tactical choices.

The current announcement archive includes 2026 interface and action-timing changes and built-in multiboxing. This proposal independently chooses one actively controlled adventurer at a time; it does not assume RWK currently shares that policy. [R4]

## Kings of Chaos

The official guide describes an army game with specialized offensive, defensive and covert capabilities, equipment and repair, conquest targets, rankings and commander/officer relationships. The same guide describes game turns accumulating by time and soldier income per turn. [R5]

**Design inference:** inherit the social dependencies and competition between different capabilities. Adapt them into player companies, scouting, fortifying routes, supply objectives and frontier charters. Replace time-produced armies and income with followers rescued on expeditions, contract-earned supplies and active projects. Avoid recruitment pyramids that make social reach stronger than play.

## Torn

The official wiki describes attacks involving individual combat decisions, bounded fight length and different outcomes after victory. Its faction chain system rewards coordinated successful attacks. [R6, R7]

**Design inference:** retain fight reports, reputations, rivalries and visible cooperative goals. Replace forced hospitalization and timer-pressure chains with consent-based conflicts and encounter-count contribution milestones. Do not import regenerating energy as a limit on play.

## SimpleMMO

The developer presents a lightweight text RPG built around adventure steps, alongside PvP, guilds, world bosses, loot, quests and a market. Its own page explicitly advertises offline progression. [R8]

**Design inference:** its compact presentation supports the idea that a browser game can feel social and substantial without character animation. Use concise encounter text and optional monster/item art. Replace random step spam and offline growth with curated encounter nodes and active choices.

## Fallen Sword

Official-domain wiki material describes stamina used for movement, fighting, buffs and group actions, with time-based recovery. The publisher's item guide also exposes stamina-related item properties. Direct retrieval of some guide pages returned 403; no live-game or current-rate verification was possible. [R9, R10]

**Design inference:** it is a useful warning about tying exploration and combat to a waiting resource. Keep equipment interactions and group encounters as design directions, but do not use hourly stamina, premium refills or one-hit farming as the desired play pattern. No claim of current balance is needed to make that decision.

## LitRPG references

The Mage Tank publisher synopsis explicitly emphasizes deliberate build decisions, durability investment, dungeon progression, humor and consequences. [R11]

The Dungeon Crawler Carl publisher descriptions establish a system-run dungeon spectacle, achievements and changing floor challenges. [R12]

**Design inference:** use visible advancement, unusual interactions, a responsive reward voice and encounters with changing rules. Multiplayer needs predictable eligibility, bounded rewards and repeatable fairness. A novel protagonist's exceptional power or preferential treatment should not become a requirement for enjoying this game.

## Comparison matrix — recommendations, not source claims

| Reference | Useful ingredient | Proposed translation | Replaced ingredient |
|---|---|---|---|
| RWK | Short hunting loop and shared world | Select a threat, make several choices, improve gear | Opaque formulas and repetitive low-information farming |
| KoC | Alliances and specialized competition | Companies with scouting, supply and defense objectives | Time-generated turns, population and gold |
| Torn | Consequential rivalry and coordinated attacks | Fight reports, objective campaigns and shared milestones | Forced downtime and energy gating |
| SimpleMMO | Readable text and broad social content | Mobile-friendly cards and encounter journal | Offline advancement and step-spam pacing |
| Fallen Sword | Equipment-centered encounters | Tag-based item interactions and selected targets | Stamina waiting and one-hit progression as the goal |
| Mage Tank / DCC | Intentional builds and comic system feedback | Original reward administrator and authored achievement rules | Protagonist privilege and copied story elements |
