# Relaxed grinding with optional depth

Current direction: RWK-style one-exchange-per-tap combat, comfortable repetition, mobile-first controls and voluntary optimization. The intermediate proposal that one click should resolve an entire ordinary fight is superseded.

## A comfortable session

Select Chainback Crabs, equip a build and tap Attack, Cast or a combined action through the fight. A suitable prepared character can repeat the same action against familiar prey. Read a short result after each exchange. On victory, receive XP/materials and continue when desired. Review an upgrade, recover or switch targets at useful intervals.

The player remains active through deliberate taps, but does not need a fresh tactical decision each time. Stronger foes and marked adventures reward more planning and changes of action. Both experiences use the same basic controls and character math.

## Where depth lives

- Before grinding: select desired materials or a level band; prepare attributes, gear and equipped abilities.
- During ordinary grinding: repeat a familiar action, with occasional recovery, upgrade review or target changes.
- At adventure checkpoints: choose a route, risk or use of limited supplies.
- In difficult fights: time defenses, interrupts and resource use through the same action interface.
- When optimizing: compare matchups, refine interactions and seek optional challenges.

Normal hunting grants eligible XP, mastery and useful loot. There is no reward multiplier for changing buttons often, selecting more menu options or inspecting every calculation. Optimization should offer satisfying improvements without making a sensible casual build feel ineffective.

## Keep the LitRPG promise

An unusual Mage Tank-inspired build should reveal its identity in ordinary combat. A result can show a defensive effect triggering a more powerful spell. Equipped interactions can resolve within one chosen action. The player need not select a complex rotation for every crab.

Discoveries, level decisions, gear interactions and authored system commentary provide moments worth attention. Routine mastery updates and common loot do not need separate dialogs. The mobile interface should let the player review progress when ready.

## Boundaries

One tap authorizes one exchange, not a whole fight or later exchanges. No auto-repeat, held-input farming, timers, queues, offline gains or programmable hunt profiles. A few familiar buttons are enough for routine combat. Relaxed repetition is permitted and must not be treated as suspicious by itself.

## What to test

Can a new player enjoy routine hunts without frequent menus? Does a harder enemy create useful action changes? Can an optimizer improve efficiency while casual leveling stays comfortable? Does the same math explain both? Are recovery, inventory and messages interrupting too often? Is the action bar comfortable and stable on a portrait phone?

Exact pacing and optimization advantages remain rough balancing questions. Mobile-first and one exchange per tap are agreed requirements.
