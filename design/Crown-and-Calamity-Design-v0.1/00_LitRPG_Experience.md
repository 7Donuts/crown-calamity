# LitRPG experience — a core requirement

Status: creative and mechanical goals, not locked features. The Mage Tank and Dungeon Crawler Carl references should shape the solo experience before the design expands into multiplayer.

## Mage Tank: the build is part of the story

The publisher's description of Mage Tank emphasizes intentional build choices, a deliberately durable character, humor and consequences. This proposal takes those as design directions, not as a specification to copy its exact mechanics. Source: https://aethonbooks.com/book/mage-tank/

The important player fantasy is: “I made an unusual choice, discovered a useful interaction, and now my character handles a problem differently.” The first ten levels should deliver a small version of this.

### A durable-caster test case

At the start, the player invests in Vitality, Will and Intellect instead of maximizing spell damage. Their health and resources improve; ordinary spells deal less damage than a dedicated damage caster's. They use a main-hand rod with a focus tag and a shield rather than a stronger offensive offhand.

Later, they learn Reservoir: a successful useful Guard stores one bounded charge for the next Arc Bolt. This turns a defensive decision into an offensive opportunity. The charge requires an actual hostile hit, cannot stack and expires quickly. In tactical encounters, choosing when to absorb pressure matters. In routine hunts, equipped effects resolve as part of the chosen basic or combined action. A familiar action can be repeated without opening an ability menu each exchange. Waiting or taking self-inflicted damage does not help.

The proposed numbers are intentionally modest. A +10% charge may not be enough to make the build feel distinct; test it. If it fails, try an alternative such as a cheaper next spell or a small barrier tied to the cast. Do not implement all alternatives at once. No damage-reflection farm, invulnerability loop or unlimited conversion of maximum HP into attack power.

Success means this build wins through a recognizable interaction and has a meaningful weakness, such as lower steady damage or reduced evasion. Normal hunt reports can demonstrate the interaction; the player need not select a different sequence of abilities each fight; repeated familiar combined actions can express the build. A large HP number alone does not satisfy the fantasy.

### Other ways to make a story-worthy build

A precision character prepares an opening before a powerful strike. A heavy-weapon character disrupts machinery that an investigator can instead disable. A spellknife switches channels against a guarded enemy. These are goals to compare with the first build, not a requirement to create several complete progression trees now.

## The system should notice what happened

After an interesting interaction, show an earned, concise response:

> **Interaction discovered: Stored Pressure.** Your Guard enabled Reservoir, strengthening the next Arc Bolt.
>
> **B.U.R.S.A.R.:** “The shield is not an approved charging station. Your results have been forwarded to Procurement.”

“Discovered” means the player has seen an existing, inspectable rule work. It does not award secret permanent power to the first person who guesses a developer's trick. A codex records the interaction and explains it clearly afterward.

The reward voice is an original bureaucratic persona. It can recognize a narrow survival, a rescued worker, a successful interruption or an unconventional tactic. It should never lie about numbers, hide mandatory instructions in sarcasm, or randomly invent rewards. Routine hunts use concise summaries; noteworthy events, not every kill, deserve an interruption.

## Status screens should make growth tangible

LitRPG progression belongs in the ordinary UI: a four-point stat allocation with visible consequences, mastery advancing toward a new behavior, a comparison that reveals a new interaction, and an achievement remembering what the character actually did.

A level-up is a short personal moment, not just a toast. Show what changed and what decision is now available. Avoid stopping play for several disconnected reward pop-ups.

## DCC as a second influence

The publisher descriptions of Dungeon Crawler Carl connect achievements, dungeon challenges and an intrusive system presence. The design borrows the broad idea that the dungeon can recognize and comment on player actions, while using original fiction, jokes and challenge structures. Source: https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/series/43C/dungeon-crawler-carl/

The solo game needs legible rules and fair rewards even when its fictional administrator is unreliable. Comedy may frame the challenge; it cannot excuse arbitrary punishment or a hidden solution.

## Add these to the first-slice acceptance criteria

- Before E5, the player makes a stat or equipment choice that visibly changes an encounter.
- Before E10, at least one supported build has a recognizable interaction between two actions or an action and an item/passive.
- An encounter can be described as a short story with a problem, a player response and a consequence.
- The narrator comments on something the player actually did, using a recorded event.
- The journal remembers at least one decision and one build interaction.
- The player can inspect the mechanics; discovery adds satisfaction, not permanent obscurity.

These goals remain part of the core solo design. They are not postponed until a future content expansion.
