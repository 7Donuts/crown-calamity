# Gear economy

The outfitter has 145 items: 75 weapons (25 per scaling channel) and ten each of the offhand, head, body, hands, feet, accessory, and relic categories. Existing item IDs remain save-format identifiers for ownership, loadouts, drops, recipes, and item modifications. Catalog names and descriptions use found toy parts, sewing supplies, soft fabric, and other objects a stuffie could carry Underfoot.

Items have a content tier and a chapter. These labels never restrict equipping owned gear. Chapter stock opens when the player claims the preceding chapter’s finale receipt; a purchase preview names the required chapter. A character may still equip, modify, or sell owned gear from a later chapter. No item uses a character-level equip requirement.

Weapon tiers 1–4 are Chapter 1, tiers 5–10 Chapter 2, tiers 11–15 Chapter 3, tiers 16–20 Chapter 4, and tiers 21–25 Chapter 5. Each channel rises monotonically in effective primary attack power. Weapon sticker prices retain the geometric curve `round(100 × 100^((tier − 1) / 9))`: tier 1 costs 100 coins, tier 10 costs 10,000, and tier 25 costs about 21.5 million. Weapon bases grow in modest steps; existing upgrades, affixes, focus tags, shields, and two-handed loadout rules remain in effect.

Offhands, head, body, hands, feet, and accessories each keep ten options with explicit, unique tiers 1–10. Two tiers become available per chapter. Their price is `round(category base × 3.2^(tier − 1))`; category bases range from 14 to 24 coins. The steeper category curve makes filling a complete regional loadout a separate savings goal from buying one weapon. Existing item IDs and combat bonuses stay stable.

Update: relic prices, slots and the relic engine now follow [design 43](43_Relic_Engine.md); the relic price ladder below is historical. The ten relics retain their separate, authored effects; they are not weapon-style upgrades. Two relics become available per chapter, priced at 500, 1,200, 3,500, 8,000, 20,000, 50,000, 125,000, 300,000, 750,000, and 1,800,000 coins. Unique effects remain useful at any chapter, and the three echo sockets strengthen their value within the existing cap. Relic socket unlocks cost 5,000, 25,000, and 125,000 coins and require Chapters 2, 3, and 4 respectively. Previously paid unlocks, active echoes, and per-socket licenses remain valid when saves are loaded.

Normal drops use `lootEligible` alongside chapter and tier. Starter kit gear, the highest weapon tier in each chapter and channel, and all unique relics are excluded. Common regional gear remains eligible. Resale uses bounded regional salvage receipts of 3, 6, 11, 20, or 38 coins in Chapters 1–5, with at most three additional coins for upgrades. Those values do not follow the exponential purchase curve. Crafted outputs keep recipe sale caps, including one coin for fragment-forged relics, so buying, upgrading, crafting, and selling cannot create a coin profit loop.

Equipment upgrades still cap at three ranks and use the existing materials. Coin costs for the three ranks begin at 35, 70, and 120 in Chapter 1, then scale by 4× per chapter. This keeps late-game refinement as a meaningful gold sink without changing the upgrade benefit or invalidating completed upgrades. Affix costs and all material save keys remain stable.

The twenty Chapter 2–5 encounter materials now have stable display labels. Their regional workshop exchanges and their use as local upgrade/reforge inputs remain pending balance work; the campaign preview does not imply that these drops already refine equipment.

Every item ID maps to a crop from the existing generated item atlas in `dist/item-art.js`. Runtime art uses only those generated raster families; new catalog entries do not introduce code-drawn art. The relic IDs and effects remain defined by `design/31_Relic_Resonance.md`.
